## What is Keloid & Hypertrophic Scar Risk Assessor?

The Keloid & Hypertrophic Scar Risk Assessor is a free clinical risk estimation tool developed by Poli International for tattoo and piercing studios. It evaluates six weighted factors to estimate a client's likelihood of developing raised scar tissue after a body modification procedure.

The tool calculates a risk score from 0 to 29 points based on:
- Personal history of raised scars (0–10 points)
- Family history of keloids (0–6 points)
- Fitzpatrick skin phototype (0–4 points)
- Intended procedure location (0–6 points)
- Current age (0–3 points)
- Prior piercing or tattoo reactions (0–6 points)

Results are presented in four tiers: Low (green), Moderate (yellow), Elevated (orange), and High (red), each with specific guidance for proceeding with the procedure.

## Who Should Use This Tool

- **Piercing clients** considering any new body piercing, especially cartilage, chest, or back placements
- **Tattoo clients** concerned about scar formation, particularly those with darker skin or family history
- **Studio artists and piercers** screening clients before procedures to identify high-risk individuals
- **Individuals with prior keloids** evaluating whether to pursue additional body modifications
- **Clients with family history** of keloid scarring who want to understand their personal risk
- **Dermatology patients** seeking preliminary risk information before consulting a specialist

## How to Use

1. **Select personal scar history**, Choose from "No history of raised scars" (0 points), "One raised scar that flattened over time" (4 points), or "Persistent or growing keloid at any prior site" (10 points).

2. **Indicate family history**, Select "No known family history" (0 points), "One parent or sibling with keloids" (3 points), or "Multiple relatives affected" (6 points).

3. **Choose Fitzpatrick skin type**, Pick "Type I–II" (0 points), "Type III–IV" (2 points), or "Type V–VI" (4 points).

4. **Select intended location**, Choose "Earlobe, nose, eyebrow, lip, or other facial site" (0 points), "Ear cartilage, navel, tongue, or upper arm" (3 points), or "Chest, upper back, shoulder, or jawline" (6 points).

5. **Enter age range**, Select "Under 25" (3 points), "25–40" (2 points), or "Over 40" (0 points).

6. **Describe prior reactions**, Choose "No prior procedure, or healed with no raised tissue" (0 points), "Had a bump that resolved over several months" (3 points), or "Persistent raised scar or still-present bump" (6 points).

7. **Click "Calculate My Risk"**, The button activates once all six factors are selected.

8. **Review your results**, The tool displays your risk tier, score, percentage, material recommendations, and warning signs.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### 1. What is the difference between a keloid and a hypertrophic scar?
A hypertrophic scar stays within the wound boundaries and may flatten over time. A keloid grows beyond the wound margin, persists without treatment, and often recurs after removal.

### 2. Can this tool diagnose keloids?
No. This tool provides an educational risk estimate only. It cannot predict keloid formation with certainty. A dermatologist must confirm any diagnosis.

### 3. What does my risk score mean?
Scores range from 0 to 29. Low risk (0–5) means standard protocols apply. Moderate (6–12) indicates discussing history with your piercer. Elevated (13–20) suggests dermatology consultation. High (21–29) indicates significant predisposition requiring specialist guidance.

### 4. Why does Fitzpatrick skin type matter?
Keloid incidence is approximately 15 times higher in Fitzpatrick types V–VI compared to types I–II. This is a population-level risk factor, not a personal certainty.

### 5. What are the highest-risk body locations?
Chest, upper back, shoulders, and jawline carry the highest risk for keloid formation. Earlobes and facial sites carry lower risk.

### 6. Why does age affect keloid risk?
Keloid formation peaks between ages 10–30 and declines significantly after 40. Hormonal activity during puberty and pregnancy further elevates risk.

### 7. What materials should I choose if I'm at elevated risk?
Implant-grade materials that minimize ongoing tissue stress are critical. BioFlex® polymer flexes with the body, reducing chronic irritation at vulnerable sites. Rigid metal can create microtrauma that triggers keloid initiation.

### 8. Can I still get pierced if I'm at high risk?
You can, but a dermatology consultation is strongly recommended first. Avoid chest, upper back, and cartilage placements. Consider pressure therapy or silicone sheeting protocols.

### 9. How accurate is this risk assessment?
This tool uses established clinical risk factors but cannot predict individual outcomes. Keloid formation involves complex genetic and environmental factors not fully captured by any screening tool.

### 10. What should I do if I develop a raised bump after piercing?
Monitor the site. A firm lump growing beyond the wound margin may be a keloid. Persistent itching, pain, or growth after 3 months warrants early dermatology review. Document with photos.
